\begin{abstract}
We present an implementation  and application of periodic real-time 
time-dependent density functional theory
(RT-TDDFT) in the real-space multigrid code \texttt{RMG} to
collinear spin-polarized systems. The formulation describes homogeneous
optical response in the long-wavelength limit using a current-based
velocity-gauge framework under periodic boundary conditions, with Bloch
$\mathbf{k}$-point and spin resolution, nonlocal-pseudopotential contributions
to the current operator, and finite active-space density-matrix propagation.
The implementation combines real-space domain decomposition with
$\mathbf{k}$-point, spin-channel, and distributed-matrix parallelism,
enabling RT-TDDFT calculations for large periodic supercells. We validate the
method  a one-dimensional periodic hydrogen-dimer model, monolayer hBN, bulk Si,
and spin-polarized
\ch{CrI3}, obtaining consistent principal spectral features across systems of
increasing dimensional and spin complexity. As a large-scale scientific
application, we investigate negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy centers in
diamond. The calculations reproduce the  polarization selection
behavior and minority-spin character of the defect-associated optical
response, reveal its dependence on the propagated active space and defect
concentration, and show how local spin selectivity maps onto global collinear
spin channels for different magnetic configurations. A single NV$^{-}$ center
in a 4096-site diamond framework containing 4095 atoms demonstrates the
applicability of the approach to dilute defects in large periodic supercells
and establishes a foundation for future extensions toward finite-momentum
probes and more general electron-ion and spin dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Summary and Outlook}
\label{sec:summary}

This work establishes a periodic and collinear spin-polarized extension
of the scalable \texttt{RMG} RT-TDDFT framework previously developed
for finite systems.\cite{jakowski-rmg-tddft-2025}  The formulation
treats homogeneous optical response in the long-wavelength
$\mathbf q\rightarrow0$ limit using a current-based velocity-gauge
description under periodic boundary conditions.  Within this framework,
the response is resolved by Bloch wave vector and collinear spin
channel, the current operator includes the contribution from
norm-conserving nonlocal pseudopotentials, and the time evolution is
performed by finite active-space density-matrix propagation.  The
implementation combines real-space multigrid domain decomposition with
k-point, spin-channel, and distributed-matrix parallelism, providing a
practical route to RT-TDDFT calculations for large periodic supercells.
The spectra reported in the present workflow are interpreted as optical
or absorption-like spectra in arbitrary units, with the formal
normalization of current response distinguished from the implemented
spectral processing.

The implementation was validated against independent \texttt{Octopus}
RT-TDDFT calculations through a progression of benchmarks:
a one-dimensional periodic hydrogen-dimer reference, pristine monolayer
hBN, bulk Si, and spin-polarized \ch{CrI3}.  Across this sequence, RMG
reproduces the principal spectral features of the independent
calculations, including major peak positions, dominant spectral
structures, overall spectral envelopes, and polarization or spin trends
where applicable.  These comparisons validate the periodic propagation,
current-response, k-point, and collinear spin-polarized workflows at a
comparable semilocal-DFT level.  They are not intended as tests of
absolute optical intensity, quantitatively normalized conductivity, or
many-body excitonic and spin-orbit effects beyond the present
semilocal, collinear treatment.

The NV$^{-}$ center in diamond provides the principal large-scale
scientific demonstration.  The calculations reproduce the expected
polarization selection behavior of the defect-associated optical
response: the feature near the experimental 1.945 eV ZPL reference is
strongly activated for transverse polarization and suppressed for
polarization parallel to the defect axis.  The spin-resolved spectra
identify the corresponding response with the local minority-spin
optical channel in the global collinear spin representation, without
invoking spin-flip or noncollinear dynamics.  Active-space tests show
that both the propagated virtual manifold and the retained occupied
manifold measurably affect the defect-associated feature, motivating a
controlled concentration study in a common 4096-site diamond framework.
For 64, 8, and 1 NV$^{-}$ centers, the spectra evolve from a fragmented
high-concentration response, consistent with stronger NV--NV coupling,
toward a simpler single-defect response as the NV--NV separation
increases.  The single-NV calculation contains 4095 actual atoms and an
approximately 28.58~\AA\ periodic image separation, approaching the
dilute-defect limit.  Comparison with independent-particle
\texttt{LOPTICS} spectra provides additional perspective on the
relation between the underlying Kohn--Sham transition structure and the
self-consistent RT-TDDFT response, and the FM/G-AFM calculations show
how the same local minority-spin optical selection rule maps
differently onto global spin channels in distinct collinear magnetic
configurations.

A natural next direction is to broaden the external perturbations
beyond the homogeneous optical limit summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:external-field-map}.  Finite-$\mathbf q$ longitudinal
scalar perturbations would provide a route to momentum-resolved density
response, EELS-type loss spectra, electron-beam-induced electronic
excitation, and plasmon dispersion.  Spatially structured scalar or
vector perturbations would also connect to plasmonic excitation and
energy-transfer problems, where the spatial profile of the perturbation
can be as important as its frequency
content.\cite{JCTC-2020,tao-2021}  Finite-$\mathbf q$ transverse
electromagnetic perturbations and multipolar or chiroptical response
represent related extensions, but they require operator and
postprocessing developments beyond the homogeneous current-response
workflow implemented here.

A second direction is to enlarge the dynamical degrees of freedom.  The
present calculations propagate electronic density matrices at fixed
nuclear positions and within a fixed collinear spin basis.  Introducing
explicitly time-dependent moving charged-projectile potentials would
extend the framework toward ion-beam excitation and electronic
stopping,\cite{theo-comp-chem-DBL-2022} while coupling the electronic
propagation self-consistently to nuclear or projectile motion would
enable Ehrenfest-type electron-ion dynamics and broader nonadiabatic
applications.  Such developments connect naturally with broader efforts
to describe coupled electronic and nuclear quantum
dynamics.\cite{Garashchuk-Jakowski-Rassolov-2026}  More general spin
dynamics would require noncollinear spin propagation, spin-orbit
coupling, and time-dependent magnetic perturbations, opening a path
toward transverse spin response and magnetic excitations in materials
where such physics is essential.  The present work therefore provides a
periodic, spin-resolved RT-TDDFT foundation in \texttt{RMG} for future
extensions toward finite-momentum probes, structured perturbations,
coupled electron-ion dynamics, and more general spin dynamics.
