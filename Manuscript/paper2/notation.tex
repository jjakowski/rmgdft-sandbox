%% ============================================================
%% UNIT SYSTEM
%% ============================================================
%% UPDATED 2026-09-24: author-approved convention; see DECISIONS.md.
%%
%% THEORY: Gaussian electromagnetic units, with dimensional constants explicit.
%% Retain hbar, electron mass m_e, elementary charge magnitude e > 0,
%% and speed of light c throughout the derivations. Electron charge q_e = -e.
%% Do NOT set these constants to numerical values mid-derivation.
%% This makes charge signs, dimensions, and factors of c traceable.
%%
%% Gaussian electromagnetic relations:
%%   kinetic momentum: p - (q_e/c) A = p + (e/c) A
%%   electric field:   E = -grad(Phi) - (1/c) dA/dt
%%   magnetic field:   B = curl(A)
%%   potential energy of an electron: q_e Phi
%% Factors of c occur in orbital electromagnetic coupling as well as
%% relativistic and spin-orbit terms. They must not be silently absorbed
%% into A or a numerical perturbation coefficient.
%%
%% IMPLEMENTATION: explicitly switch to Hartree atomic units at its start.
%%   hbar = m_e = e = 1; q_e = -1.
%% Retain the Gaussian electromagnetic convention:
%%   c = 1/alpha approximately 137.036; c is NOT set to 1.
%% Any code variable representing a scaled vector potential (e.g. A/c)
%% or integrated perturbation strength must be defined separately.
%%
%% Atomic-unit reference quantities (Gaussian expressions):
%%   a_0 = hbar^2/(m_e e^2) = 1 bohr
%%   E_h = e^2/a_0         = 1 Hartree, approximately 27.211 eV
%%   t_0 = hbar/E_h        = 1 atomic unit of time, approximately 24.19 as
%%   alpha = e^2/(hbar c)
%% The often-used condition 4*pi*epsilon_0 = 1 describes Hartree atomic
%% units in SI notation. epsilon_0 does not enter the Gaussian equations
%% used in this manuscript; do not mix SI and Gaussian field formulas.
%%
%% Explicit constants support dimensional checks, for example:
%%   kinetic energy: -hbar^2 grad^2/(2m_e)
%%   propagation exponent: a Hamiltonian time integral divided by hbar
%%   Liouville-von Neumann equation: i*hbar*dP/dt = [H,P]
%%
%% Experimental values and plotted quantities may use explicitly stated
%% practical units, such as eV, angstrom, or fs, with consistent conversions.
