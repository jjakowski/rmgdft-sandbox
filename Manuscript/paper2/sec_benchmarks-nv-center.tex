\section[Large-Scale Demonstration: NV- Centers in Diamond]{Large-Scale Demonstration: NV$^{-}$ Centers in Diamond}
\label{sec:nv}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

As a final scientific application, we apply the periodic collinear
spin-polarized RT-TDDFT implementation to negatively charged
nitrogen-vacancy centers (NV$^{-}$) in diamond.  The NV$^{-}$ center is
one of the most widely studied solid-state point defects because its
spin-triplet ground state is optically addressable and its long
coherence times make it useful for quantum sensing, single-photon
emission, and quantum information
applications.\cite{NV-Doherty-2013,NV-Gali-2008,NV-Gali-2019,NV-coherence-Balasubramanian-2009,NV-sensing-Degen-2017,NV-sensing-Maze-2008,NV-sensing-Schirhagl-2014,NV-spe-Aharonovich-2016,NV-QComp-Bradley-2019}
For the present work, the NV$^{-}$ center also provides a stringent
large-scale test of the method: it combines localized defect states,
spin-polarized electronic structure, polarization-resolved optical
selection rules, active-space convergence requirements, and long-range
periodic image effects in a three-dimensional
host.\cite{NV-Gali-2008,NV-Gali-2019,NV-Louie-2012}

The goal of this section is not to provide a complete quantitative
description of NV$^{-}$ photophysics.  The experimental zero-phonon
line (ZPL) involves excited-state relaxation, vibronic coupling, and
electron--phonon effects that are outside the fixed-ion adiabatic-PBE
response considered here.\cite{NV-Doherty-2013,NV-Gali-2019}
Instead, the experimental $E_{\mathrm{ZPL}}=1.945$~eV is used as a
reference energy for the defect-associated optical
response.\cite{Davies-Hamer-NV-ZPL-1976,NV-Doherty-2013}
We first establish that the calculation reproduces the expected local
defect orbital character, polarization selection rule, and spin-channel
selectivity.  We then examine active-space dependence and use a common
4096-site diamond framework to study how the optical spectrum evolves
from concentrated periodic NV$^{-}$ arrays toward the dilute-defect
limit.

All NV$^{-}$ calculations reported here used \texttt{RMG} with
periodic boundary conditions, the PBE exchange-correlation functional,
and the \texttt{NC\_accuracy} norm-conserving pseudopotentials.  The
bulk diamond lattice constant was optimized with the same functional
and pseudopotentials, giving $a_{\rm diamond}=3.5725125$~\AA, and was
then held fixed for the defect calculations.  The negatively charged
defect cells were treated with a uniform compensating background
charge.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Defect Electronic Structure and Optical Selection Rules}
\label{sec:nv-electronic-selection}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The NV$^{-}$ center consists of a substitutional nitrogen atom adjacent
to a carbon vacancy, with the defect axis oriented along the
crystallographic $[111]$ direction.  This axis defines the $C_3$ axis
of the approximately $C_{3v}$ defect.  The defect introduces localized
states in the diamond band gap.  In the standard single-particle
description, the triplet ground state associated with the ${}^3A_2$
manifold has the occupation $a_1^2 e_x^1 e_y^1$, with the
$a_1$-like orbital and the two components of the $e$ manifold localized
primarily on the nitrogen and the three carbon atoms adjacent to the
vacancy.\cite{NV-Doherty-2013,NV-Gali-2008,NV-Gali-2019}

The optical selection rule for the defect-associated
${}^3A_2\rightarrow{}^3E$ transition provides a direct physics-based
test of the periodic spin-polarized RT-TDDFT
calculation.\cite{Gali-spin-conserving-NV-2009,NV-Gali-2019}
For the NV$^{-}$ center, the vector component parallel to the defect
axis transforms as $A_1$, whereas the two transverse components
transform as the two-dimensional $E$ representation.\cite{Maze-NV-group-theory-2011,NV-Doherty-2013}
Thus an axial perturbation does not produce an allowed electric-dipole
transition between the ${}^3A_2$ and ${}^3E$ manifolds,
\begin{equation}
  A_2 \otimes A_1 \otimes E = E,
\end{equation}
while a transverse perturbation is symmetry allowed,
\begin{equation}
  A_2 \otimes E \otimes E = A_1 \oplus A_2 \oplus E .
\end{equation}

We tested this selection rule using the 512-site NV-$x4$ supercell
containing 511 atoms after defect formation.  The spectra in
Fig.~\ref{fig:nv-selection-rules} compare an impulsive perturbation
parallel to the defect axis,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf e}_{\parallel}
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1,1,1),
\end{equation}
with a transverse perturbation,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf e}_{\perp}
  =
  \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1,-1,0).
\end{equation}
The defect-associated feature near the experimental ZPL reference is
strongly activated for transverse polarization and strongly suppressed
for axial polarization, consistent with the expected $C_{3v}$ optical
selection rule.

The spin-resolved response further identifies the active local spin
channel.  In the global collinear convention used in
Fig.~\ref{fig:nv-selection-rules}, the transverse defect-region feature
is dominated by the spin-down channel.  This corresponds to the local
minority-spin $a_1\rightarrow e$ excitation in the triplet defect
configuration.\cite{Gali-spin-conserving-NV-2009,NV-Gali-2019}
Because the optical perturbation is spin independent, this should not
be interpreted as a spin-flip transition; rather, it shows that the
spin-conserving response is localized in the spin channel containing
the relevant occupied defect orbital.

\subsection{Active-Space Dependence of the Optical Response}
\label{sec:nv-active-space}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The finite density-matrix propagation described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation-density-matrix} requires selecting an
active subspace of ground-state Kohn--Sham orbitals.  This choice is
especially important for large defect supercells, where including all
occupied and a large number of unoccupied states can become
prohibitively expensive.  We therefore examined the active-space
dependence of the defect-associated optical response using the same
NV-$x4$ supercell and transverse polarization as in
Fig.~\ref{fig:nv-selection-rules}.

The NV-$x4$ ground-state calculation has 1024 occupied states.  The
reference active space retained all occupied states and included
3200 unoccupied states, corresponding to 6.25 virtual states per
pristine diamond site in the 512-site cell.  The virtual-space tests
used 512, 1024, 2048, and 3200 propagated unoccupied states,
corresponding to 1, 2, 4, and 6.25 virtual states per site.  The
occupied-space tests varied the RMG starting-state index,
\texttt{tddft\_start\_state}, over the values 0, 200, 400, 512, 600,
and 800.  Since this index defines the lower boundary of the propagated
orbital subspace, the corresponding numbers of retained occupied
states are 1024, 824, 624, 512, 424, and 224.

Figure~\ref{fig:nv-active-space} shows that the defect-region feature
is sensitive to both parts of the active manifold.  Increasing the
number of propagated unoccupied states produces a systematic redshift
of the defect-associated feature that gradually saturates as the active
space is enlarged, indicating convergence with respect to the
virtual-state manifold over the tested range.  Truncating the occupied
manifold shifts the response to higher energy, with the largest change
occurring for the most aggressive occupied-space truncation.  These
results show that the localized defect excitation is not fully
described by a minimal defect-only active space; the surrounding
diamond environment and the response of the occupied manifold
contribute measurably to the calculated optical feature.

This active-space behavior motivated the design of the final
large-supercell concentration study below.  To avoid conflating
finite-size effects with changes in the propagated active space, the
final concentration comparison was performed within a common
4096-site diamond framework using the same active-space construction.

\subsection{Defect Concentration and the Large-Supercell Limit}
\label{sec:nv-concentration-large-cell}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

To isolate the effect of NV--NV separation from changes in overall cell
size, we constructed a concentration series within the same nominal
4096-site diamond framework.  The three systems contain 64, 8, and
1 NV$^{-}$ centers, respectively.  The 64-NV system forms a regular
$4\times4\times4$ simple-cubic lattice of NV-center nitrogen positions
embedded in the diamond host, with a nearest-neighbor NV--NV spacing of
approximately $7.15$~\AA, or $2a_{\rm diamond}$.  The 8-NV system is
obtained by taking every other site of this defect lattice, giving a
$2\times2\times2$ simple-cubic NV array with spacing
approximately $14.29$~\AA, or $4a_{\rm diamond}$.  The single-defect
periodic system has one NV$^{-}$ center in the 4096-site host, giving
4095 actual atoms after replacing one carbon by nitrogen and removing a
nearest-neighbor carbon atom.  Its periodic image separation is
approximately $28.58$~\AA, or $8a_{\rm diamond}$.

All three final concentration calculations used the same starting-state
index of 2700 and 8200 unoccupied states.  Because the systems contain
different numbers of electrons, the total propagated active-space
dimension is not exactly identical in every case, but these choices
produce comparable propagated subspaces with dimensions of approximately
$1.37\times10^4$ states.  The 8200 unoccupied states correspond to
roughly two virtual states per pristine diamond site.  The RT-TDDFT
trajectories were propagated for at least 42000 time steps with
$\Delta t=1.0$~a.u., corresponding to approximately 1~ps of real-time
propagation.

Figure~\ref{fig:nv-concentration-ipa} compares the self-consistent
RT-TDDFT spectra with independent-particle approximation (IPA) spectra
computed using the \texttt{LOPTICS} implementation in RMG.  For each
corresponding concentration case, the IPA and RT-TDDFT calculations use
the same geometry, magnetic configuration, $k$-point setup, and
ground-state electronic structure.  This provides a direct comparison
between the independent-particle and self-consistent real-time
responses for the same underlying electronic structure.  The IPA
spectra expose the underlying Kohn--Sham transition structure, while
the RT-TDDFT spectra include the induced time-dependent Hartree and
adiabatic exchange-correlation response.  The IPA spectra are not
treated as an exact reference.

The concentration dependence is pronounced.  For the 64-NV array, the
defect-region spectral weight is redistributed over several nearby
features around the experimental ZPL reference.  This fragmentation is
consistent with stronger NV--NV coupling at the higher defect
concentration, where the characteristic separation is only
$\sim 7.15$~\AA.  At the 8-NV concentration, where the NV--NV spacing
is doubled, the defect-region response is less fragmented.  In the
single-NV 4095-atom calculation, where the periodic image separation is
approximately $28.58$~\AA, the defect-region response becomes
substantially simpler and approaches the dilute-defect limit.  The
comparison demonstrates that large real-space cells are needed to
separate the intrinsic local defect response from the
finite-concentration spectral structure of periodic NV arrays.

\subsection{Effect of Collinear Magnetic Configuration}
\label{sec:nv-magnetic-configuration}
%--------------------------------------------------------------------

The fixed-volume NV arrays also allow us to examine how the global
collinear magnetic configuration affects the spin-resolved optical
response.  We considered parallel FM and G-type antiferromagnetic
(G-AFM) broken-symmetry initial configurations for the 64- and 8-NV
arrays in the 4096-site host.  For the 64-NV system, the G-AFM
configuration alternates the local spin orientation between neighboring
sites of the effective $4\times4\times4$ simple-cubic NV lattice.  The
8-NV system uses the analogous alternating pattern on the reduced
$2\times2\times2$ defect lattice.  These configurations are used as
controlled collinear spin arrangements; no claim is made here that
G-AFM is the equilibrium magnetic ground state of an NV array.

The interpretation follows from the local minority-spin selection rule
identified for the single NV$^{-}$ center.  In the FM configuration,
all NV centers have the same local spin orientation, so the optically
active local minority-spin transition maps predominantly onto one
global spin channel.  Contributions from equivalent NV centers then
accumulate in that same global channel.  In the G-AFM configuration,
neighboring defects have opposite local spin orientations.  The local
minority-spin transition therefore maps onto the spin-up global channel
on one magnetic sublattice and the spin-down global channel on the
other.  As a result, each global spin channel receives contributions
from approximately half of the NV centers.

This behavior is visible in Fig.~\ref{fig:nv-fm-gafm}.  For a fixed
defect concentration, the dominant FM response in its active global
spin channel is roughly twice the response in either individual G-AFM
global spin channel, consistent with the redistribution expected from
the two magnetic sublattices.  This is a qualitative-to-semiquantitative
interpretation of the plotted spectra, whose curves were processed in
the same way for the FM and G-AFM cases.  The result does not imply a
spin-flip optical transition: the optical perturbation remains spin
independent, and the observed redistribution reflects the mapping
between local minority spin and the fixed global collinear spin basis.

Together, the NV$^{-}$ calculations show that the periodic
spin-polarized RT-TDDFT implementation captures the local optical
selection rules and spin-channel structure of a well-characterized
defect while scaling to a 4095-atom single-defect diamond calculation.
The concentration series demonstrates how the defect-region optical
spectrum evolves as NV--NV coupling is reduced with decreasing defect
concentration and the
system approaches the dilute-defect limit, and the FM/G-AFM comparison
shows how local spin selectivity is represented in global collinear
spin channels for extended defect arrays.
